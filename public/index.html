Questions, answered

Is it free?

Yes. No account, no sign-in, no ads. Type a name and it plays.

Where do the numbers come from?

GitHub's own API: the same contribution graph you see on a profile, plus the commits on each repository's default branch.

Why doesn't it match my profile exactly?

The story counts from 1 January to today. Profiles can also show private contributions you've chosen to share, which public mode only sees as a total. The coding clock and commit words use your latest 100 commits in each of your top six repositories.

Do you keep my data?

Public results are cached for six hours so a shared link opens fast, then they expire. Results made with your own token never touch our server.

What can my token see?

Only what you allow when you create it. It goes from your browser directly to api.github.com and lives in this tab until you close it.

Can I wrap my company?

Yes. Switch to Organization. Private repositories need a token from a member of that organization.

Which time zone is the coding clock in?

Yours. Commit times are shown in the time zone of the device you're watching on.

Where does the music come from?

It's made in your browser from your own year. Each week of contributions becomes a note, busier weeks play higher, and the beat builds as the story goes on, before a closing track plays on the last slide. Tap the three bars in the corner to mute it.

Is this made by GitHub?

No. It's an independent project built on GitHub's public API, not affiliated with GitHub or Spotify.
